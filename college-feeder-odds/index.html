How much is admissions advantage?

Chetty, Deming & Friedman (NBER w31492; QJE 2026) find non-religious private applicants are admitted to Ivy-Plus colleges at about 2× the rate of applicants with the same scores and demographics from well-resourced public schools. The rest of the raw gap is pipeline: who applies and who scores high.

Score-pool check: about 3.6% of public SAT takers vs. 10–14% of private takers score 1400+, so private students are roughly 37–44% of the 1400+/1500+ pool, close to their ~39% share of Ivy seats. Conditional on scores alone, all-private vs. all-public is roughly 0.8–1.2×.

Gaps and caveats

  • No usable split: Rice, Caltech, Carnegie Mellon, Emory, Georgetown (current), Michigan, UVA, UNC and the rest of the top 50. Peers suggest private elites run ~5–8×; Catholic universities higher.
  • UC Berkeley and UCLA: a 2023 San Francisco Chronicle analysis of 2022 statewide UC data shows public and private applicants admitted at similar rates. Derived per-graduate enrollment ratio ≈1.3–1.5× for California residents. Other flagships with in-state mandates likely sit in a similar low range.
  • Year mismatch: classes run from 2022 (Notre Dame) to 2030 (Yale, WashU); all use 2022–23 denominators. Swapping in 2025 projections moves every ratio by ~3–4%.
  • Charter and magnet schools count as public, which pads the public numerator with selective schools such as Boston Latin and Thomas Jefferson.
  • Surveys: Harvard, UChicago and Northwestern shares come from voluntary surveys with roughly 33–61% response.
  • Odds ratio vs. relative rate: with probabilities under 2% they differ by under 2%.
